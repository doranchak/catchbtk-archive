Bottom line

  1. Kent T. Billingham wrote exactly one post in the recoverable archive: #1161, 11 Oct 2004, “her shoes were together sir”. The cryptic posts in your two JPGs were posted under the guest names “-” and “^”, not under Kent’s name. They sit in the same thread (“Messages from BTK”), but the archive holds no IP addresses, e-mail fields or logins that tie them to Kent, so treating them as Kent’s is an inference. The thematic evidence for it is real but modest (section 2).
  2. Everything I could decode traces back to public material. The hex string is plain ASCII for ZKCK50photoBTK (verified). “Her shoes were together” and “face down” both appear on the site’s own inside.html page (first captured 5 Sep 2004), which says BTK’s sketch of Nancy Fox drew her “face down” and her shoes “together instead of apart”. The Dec 7–9 posts straddle the anniversary of Fox’s murder (8 Dec). “K50” is the FAA identifier of Cook Airfield in Rose Hill, Kansas (verified externally); forum members had documented Joseph Otero’s Cook Airfield job on the board since June 2004. Only “toes were pointed inward second was open an 1"” has no source I could find; it stays unresolved.
  3. The rumor has one on-the-record supporter, and no names were given. The board’s administrator, Tom Voigt, wrote on 18 Aug 2005: “SCBenson: I believe Rader posted here several times” (#14038). The statement does not say which accounts. No archived post contains a verifiably non-public fact, and Kent never answered Tom’s authentication challenge (#1222).
  4. Shortlist (all low confidence). Best of a weak field: Persona A (Kent / “-” / “^”). Then B. Kohler (a Sept 2004 post that members remembered as chilling, later removed), the “sir”/Billie the Kid circle, the T. Bob Kreisch / Christ on the Cross / ACL cluster, and two registered accounts that survive every filter I could apply (Josef_Carlin, Tippy). Ruled out or unlikely: the February 2005 “BTK” burst (its author was sitting in the live chat room), the “faucet” poster, Prairie_chicken, Puzzler, Micaela and the 184 registered usernames that kept posting after the arrest.
  5. Limits. The folder holds 11,577 unique posts, which is 91.5% of the posts the board’s own thread indexes list; about 78% of the missing 1,067 are in threads created at or after the 25 Feb 2005 arrest. “Stopped posting at the arrest” is therefore a leaky filter. Nothing here can identify an individual; the shortlist ranks plausibility, not proof.

What was searched

The folder is a Wayback Machine dump of catchbtk.com: 8,644 snapshot folders, about 5,800 files, 148 MB. The message board ran Discus software, and the same post usually appears in dozens of snapshots. I parsed every page that contains posts (2,802 pages), then de-duplicated by (post ID, timestamp), because the board re-used two post IDs after deletions. Where snapshots disagree about a post’s text (7 posts, all moderator edits) the newest version is kept and the older ones are preserved.

  • 11,577 unique posts by 656 author names in 719 threads (names that differ only by case, spacing or a repeated “(Name)” suffix are merged); 364 registered usernames, the rest guests (a guest types any name; the board keeps no username for them).
  • Coverage: the board’s own topic indexes list 12,482 posts in the threads they show; 11,415 are in this folder (91.5%). The gap is mostly post-arrest threads (e.g. “Suspect in Custody???”, 212 posts, none captured).
  • Deleted posts: 152 posts were captured early and are absent from later captures of a thread that still contains posts before and after them (usually moderator deletions). They are flagged “vanished” here and in the archive.
  • Clock: the board’s timestamps are US Pacific (verified against the Unix times stored in each page). Central time, where Wichita is, is two hours later; both are shown for the key posts.

Name search. Searching every author field, post body and thread title for Kent, Billingham, “KTB” and variants found: one post authored by “Kent T. Billingham”; one thread titled “Kent T. Billingham” in the BTK Suspects topic (10 posts); and 13 other posts that mention him.

1 · Kent T. Billingham: everything on record

1.1 The post, and the administrator’s reply

The thread “Messages from BTK” was opened by Tom Voigt on 17 Aug 2004 with the rule that only BTK should post there.

1.2 The name

“Kent T. Billingham” has the initials KTB, BTK reversed. Members noticed within a week (#1265: “the initials were KTB”) and again on 1 Dec (#1539, “first initials of kent t billingham backwards are btk”). Flytodeb (#1276) called it a phony because the sentence “neither starts with a capital letter nor ends with punctuation”. A more cautious member (#1188, 12 Oct) wrote that the post was probably “some poor sap trying to get recognition”.

Two later observations from members, both unverified: Topeka_guy (#1411, 18 Nov) said Crime Library reported the May 2004 letter was postmarked “Thomas B. King” (TBK), giving a series of BTK-letter aliases (Bill Thomas Killman, Thomas B. King, perhaps Kent T. Billingham); and Audreyamw (#7252, 13 Feb 2005) noted that Billingham is a camera-bag maker, which would fit the “photo” in the Dec 7 hex string.

1.3 Where “her shoes were together” comes from

In the “Kent T. Billingham” suspect thread, Ksguy2178 pointed to the interview on the site’s own page inside.html (#1343). I checked the archive: that page was captured on 5 Sep 2004, five weeks before Kent’s post, and says:

So the phrase was public to anyone reading the site. That does not say who typed it, but it removes the strongest reason to read it as insider knowledge. “Sir” is read two ways: as a courtesy in the style of other “Messages to BTK” posters (Castle Sander and Billie the Kid used it that same December), or as a witness answering an officer (“Where were her shoes?” — “together, sir”).

1.4 The post-arrest reading, and why I would not rely on it

Vscantu argued that “her shoes were together sir” anticipated a detail from another victim’s home that nobody then knew; Rocky corrected the victim to Delores Davis. Two problems: (1) the pyramid detail was in the news by 21 Feb 2005, but I cannot show it was unknown in October 2004, so the argument rests on an assumption; (2) “together” is not “piled in a pyramid”, while it matches the Fox sketch statement exactly. Parsimony favors the Fox page.

1.5 What the administrator later said

Oklahoma_mike (#9599, 27 Feb 2005) recalled “a post … around Sept or Oct … ‘B. something that started with a K’ … the hair stand on end … the day after both those posts were gone.” That most plausibly refers to B. Kohler (my inference: “B.” plus a K-surname), whose post and replies are flagged vanished (section 4), and not to Kent, whose post survives in 30 captures.

2 · The “-” and “^” posts in your JPGs

The two JPGs show the thread page with these posts. They are by unregistered guests named only “-” or “^”. The earlier JPG shows Kent’s post directly above the first “-” post, which is why they look like one voice, but they are separated by eight weeks. (Tom’s 14 Oct reply, #1222, sits between them in the archived captures but is not visible in the JPG.)

Are they the same hand as Kent?

Points toward a link
  • Same thread, all guest posts with symbol-only names for “-” and “^” (a habit of minimal names).
  • Same typing style as Kent: lowercase, no spaces between some words, no terminal punctuation.
  • Same subject matter: where a victim’s body and feet lay. Shoes together (Fox sketch), toes pointed inward, face up / face down (Fox “face down” on inside.html). The thread’s earliest guest post is also a Fox item, the poem “Oh! Death to Nancy” (#844, 20 Aug, later deleted).
  • The hex post is “signed” BTK, and the 7–9 Dec posts straddle the Fox anniversary (8 Dec); the repeat on 16 Dec came two days after the real 14 Dec package that contained Fox’s license.
Points against
  • Different names and no shared e-mail field (the archive does show shared e-mail addresses linking other guest names, but not these).
  • All decodable content is public (section 3).
  • The thread attracted at least a dozen other guest names, several demonstrably impostors, including some that borrowed Zodiac wording (#1896).
  • Member “hta” (#2077) says an early copy of the hex decode was deleted, so moderation was already trimming the thread.
  • The hex message is not about the Fox scene; it points at aviation codes.

Verdict: plausible single-author cluster (I call it “Persona A”), but unproven. It could equally be one enthusiast who read inside.html.

3 · Deciphering

3.1 The Dec 7 hex string — result: plain ASCII, ZKCK50photoBTK

Split into 14 byte pairs and read as ASCII, every byte is a printable letter or digit:

5A4B434B353070686F746F42544B
ZKCK50photoBTK

Fourteen consecutive bytes in the range 0x30–0x7A that all spell sensible text is far too unlikely to be chance, so the author encoded it on purpose. Interpretations, from most to least supported:

3.2 “toeswerepointedinwardsecondwasopenan1"” — result: unresolved

Word break: toes were pointed inward, second was open an 1". The final 1" is the inch mark (the typist wrote “1"” for “inch”), so the best literal reading is “Toes were pointed inward. Second [something] was open an inch.” Members read it as victim positioning (Skov, wrack, X3maine: #2435, #2490, #3189). Berkeley Street read “1"” as part of a BTK symbol (#2036), which I find unsupported.

What I could and could not establish. I searched every non-forum page in the archive and every forum post before 9 Dec 2004 for “toes”, “pointed inward”, “second … open” and “inch”: no source. The phrase fits the same “how the body and feet lay” theme as the other posts, so a victim-scene description is the natural reading. “Second [door / drawer / eye / button] open an inch” is equally literal and I cannot choose between them. If this describes a real scene, a crime-scene report would settle it; the board’s content does not.

3.3 “5A4B43 4B3530…” — result: the first half of the hex string

See 3.1: ZKC K50 with the “photoBTK” tail removed, regrouped in sixes, with an ellipsis. It looks like an author pointing readers at the location codes.

4 · If BTK did post, who might he be?

The basic problem. The archive has no IPs, and no way to test a writing sample against his. So this is a plausibility ranking from behavior, and a list of reasons to rule accounts out. What I did:

  1. Alibi filter (registered usernames only). Dennis Rader was arrested on 25 Feb 2005. Of 364 registered usernames, 184 kept posting after the arrest and are excluded; 180 did not. Guests cannot be tested because anyone can type any name. The filter is leaky because ~900 mostly post-arrest posts were not captured.
  2. Self-reported facts. Rader was born in 1945, served in the Air Force 1966–70 and was president of his church council (Britannica and other biographies); the board’s own post-arrest threads establish that he lived in Park City, north of Wichita, with a wife and children (e.g. #14038). Posters who state contradicting facts are ruled out for that reason.
  3. Behavior. Posting in the thread reserved for BTK; BTK-initial name play; puzzle-dropping; posts that track real BTK events; style continuity with the Kent cluster; any non-public fact that checks out (none found); impostor tells.
  4. The board’s own view. After the arrest, members ran a “Was BTK posting here? ROLL CALL” thread and several (Topsailgrl #10111) suspected he used “several names”. The administrator wrote “I believe Rader posted here several times”. That points to guest-name clusters more than to one registered account, which is how I weighted the list.

1 · Persona A: “Kent T. Billingham”, “-”, “^” Low–moderate (best of a weak field)

Why he might be BTK
  • Only cluster in the BTK-reserved thread that behaves like BTK’s known clue-dropping (ciphers, chapter lists, “PJ’s”): terse posts that reward decoding.
  • Kent’s name is a BTK permutation, as BTK’s real aliases reportedly were.
  • The content is thematically coherent (Fox scene) and the dates line up with the Fox anniversary and the real 14 Dec package.
  • Kent never answered Tom’s verification question, which is also what a real sender trying to avoid exposure would do.
Why not
  • Every decodable detail was public; the one unexplained phrase (“toes … inward”) is unverified.
  • Attribution of “-” and “^” to Kent is inference, not evidence.
  • The thread was full of imitators; a bored enthusiast with inside.html open fits equally well.

What would change my mind: any detail from a post that police could confirm as unreleased (the “toes … second … an inch” line is the only candidate), or Tom Voigt’s server logs showing these posts shared a connection with other BTK-thread posts.

2 · B. Kohler (guest, Messages to BTK, 27 Sep 2004) Low

Why he might be BTK
  • Oklahoma_mike (#9599) remembered a “B. … K” post that made “the hair stand on end” and then disappeared. The archive shows this post and its replies present in the 1 Oct 2004 capture and absent from the 10 Dec 2004 capture.
  • The tone is cold and certain, and the post is addressed to the other posters rather than to BTK: “he never intends to be caught and wont be … give it up”.
Why not
  • It speaks of BTK as “he”, which is what an admirer or troll would also do.
  • 43 posts in that thread (15 of them from September 2004) vanished between captures, including harmless ones from known members, so deletion is not special.
  • One post only.

What would change my mind: a follow-up under the same name, or Tom saying why the post was removed.

3 · “Sir” circle: Billie the Kid and Castle Sander Very low

Why he might be BTK
  • “Billie The Kid” is another BTK-initial persona; both posters used “sir”, run-together text and puzzle talk, around the same Dec 9 hour as the “toes” post (13:11, 14:06, 14:37).
  • Castle Sander built a working Caesar cipher and addressed BTK as a “master cat burglar”.
Why not
  • Billie the Kid says directly “i do not post from btk. there is another billy who is not me” (#2568) and mocks the use of “sir” (#2556).
  • Castle Sander’s cipher is a taunting test question (“ARE YOU THE EAST AREA RAPIST”), not a confession.

What would change my mind: evidence that “sir” was one author’s signature rather than a common politeness in that month’s “Messages to BTK”.

4 · T. Bob Kreisch / Christ on the Cross / Lightbringer / ACL Low

Why he might be BTK
  • The most elaborate “BTK voice” on the board, with deliberate acronym play (TBK; BeatTheKing e-mails).
  • SCBenson linked Kent, Kreisch and “A Criminal Lawyer / ACL” by style (#3201), and the administrator later wrote “I believe Rader posted here several times” (#14038).
Why not
  • The account of walking away from the Wegerle house contradicts the known stolen car (#2721).
  • Elaborate game-playing and fake e-mails read like a skilled imitator, not a man trying to hide.
  • “ACL” claimed to be a semi-retired criminal lawyer offering BTK counseling, which is a role-play.

What would change my mind: a verified non-public fact in any of these posts.

5 · Josef_Carlin (registered) Very low

Why he might be BTK
  • Passes the alibi filter (12 posts, 12 Dec 2004 – 10 Feb 2005, never again). Writes “I lived in the Wichita area during many of the events which surround this case”.
  • Posted a calm analysis of whether communicating with police ever helps a killer (5 Jan 2005), the question BTK was weighing that month.
Why not
  • Nothing non-public; the threads are about anagrams, Olan Mills photography and DNA.
  • Ordinary sleuth behavior, and most of the board did the same.

What would change my mind: any statement about the cases that checks out as unreleased.

6 · Tippy (registered) Very low

Why he might be BTK
  • Passes the alibi filter, 68 posts, last post 24 Feb 2005 10:32 site time, the day before the arrest.
  • Claims Kansas family roots in the Park City / Valley Center area and writes about the region’s churches and history; 20 of their 68 posts fall in the 3 am hour, site time (5 am Central), a steady early-morning routine.
Why not
  • No contradicting facts, but also no positive evidence; the 5 am Central routine and the regional knowledge are weak signals, shared by many early risers.
  • Their last post is about model railroad clubs, nothing near the case’s unreleased details.

What would change my mind: a post after the arrest (the posts in thread 579, 212 of them, were not captured).

B · Sources and methods

  • All forum quotes: this folder (websites/www.catchbtk.com/<snapshot>/discus/messages/…), parsed and de-duplicated; each quote links to the searchable archive, which links back to the saved page.
  • K50 = Cook Airfield (Rose Hill, Kansas): AirNav and other airport directories returned by a web search; none mentioned Otero.
  • Arrest date and Rader’s age, Air Force years and church role: Britannica and other biography pages returned by a web search (sources differ on 25 vs 26 Feb; I used 25 Feb).
  • The AP article “Cybersleuths Still Flock to BTK Case” (Fox News, 13 Jan 2015) quotes catchBTK administrator Angela Avey about a user who “signed in as ‘Dogcatcher’”. The article does not say that user was Rader, and no such username appears in this archive; I did not use it as evidence.
  • Cipher tests: index of coincidence; substitution hill-climbing; columnar transposition (exhaustive ≤ 8 columns, hill-climbing ≤ 18), decimation, rail-fence and Caesar/Atbash shifts, scored with a quadgram model built from the forum text. “Unsolved” means no verifiable plaintext, not that none exists.
  • Images and attachments. The board had no upload feature; posts show images, PDFs and audio only by linking to them. None of the posts quoted in this report carries an attachment. In the companion archive, the 109 posts that link to such files are filterable (“Has attachments”); the 16 files that exist in this folder are embedded inline, and the other 162 links (hosted on Photobucket, wichita.gov and similar sites) appear as tiles with the original and Wayback Machine links. Those external files were not downloaded.
  • The “vanished” flag is a heuristic (a later capture of the same thread contains posts both before and after the post but not the post itself). It cannot tell a deletion from a move to a page that was never captured.
