Associated Press

October 26, 2004
WICHITA, Kan. -

Wichita police investigating the BTK serial killings confirmed Tuesday they have obtained another letter that could be connected to the case.

Police said the letter was submitted Monday to the Federal Bureau of Investigation for authentication. Authorities released no other information about the letter.

In a series of letters from BTK - which stands for "bind, torture, kill" - the killer has claimed responsibility for eight deaths in Wichita between 1974 and 1986. The communications had stopped for more than two decades before resuming this year.

Attention has refocused on BTK since March, when The Wichita Eagle received a letter with information on an unsolved 1986 killing. The letter contained a copy of the victim's driver's license and photos of her taken after she was slain.

It was the first communication from the BTK strangler since the late 1970s, and police said it linked the killer to an eighth slaying. The other seven people were slain in the 1970s.

Another letter sent to Wichita television station KAKE was turned over to police on May 5 and was later confirmed to also be an authentic BTK communication.

That letter contained a puzzle and photocopies of employee identification cards for two men: a former Southwestern Bell worker and a former employee of the Wichita public school district. Police believe the killer may have used the IDs to gain entry to homes.

Wichita police also received a letter in June describing the 1974 strangulation of four family members - the earliest of eight deaths for which the killer known as the BTK strangler has claimed responsibility.
